A [Goliath](Goliath.md) with an inferiority complex… 

Rurik’s a glue guy who can lead with a morale-boosting, oft-crude joke or by being first in the fight and last out. He’s as skilled with his tongue as he is with his blade, but he knows, in his heart of hearts, that he’ll never live up to his Goliath ancestry… those mythical heroes of old cast long shadows over his measly existence. 

His leading by example is his way of overcompensating for his fear that he’s actually just a big, dumb brute. But his real love is decorative blacksmithing.

He doesn’t leave his party… but, he won’t party with other Goliaths. He’s left his tribe because while he’s got all the tools, he is missing that je ne sais quoi…. All tools, no carpenter.  

He was supposed to be a leader, a champion… his nomadic tribal family needs to settle down, needs stability... after a handful of failed attempts at securing a homeland, he left… declaring that he’ll make greater progress if he goes it alone, telling them that he’ll eventually return victorious… but he doesn’t believe in himself… and leaving was the simplest way to not face the letdown he brought to his people. He’s a coward, unable to beat his own mind.

He knows that the ancestors were excellent problem solvers… which is how his love of decorative blacksmithing came about… his tent was constantly being damaged by sparks flying from a poorly designed furnace. He took to solving the problem by redesigning a fireplace… while it was beautiful - with delicate curves and mini spirals designed to direct heat and trap sparks. But this failed catastrophically. They lost two tents and a child was burned from his foolishness. All form, no function. Instead he’s been smithing beautiful, yet functionally useless pieces, which he sells at markets to make ends meet…

He is scared that he’ll never be able to use his brain and brawn to bring safety to his family and to restore his own name.  

He believes that he’ll never see home or family again. He only has his party, and will do whatever it takes to keep them together.
