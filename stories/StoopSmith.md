# The Stooped Smith of Hammerkeep

The first time [Thalane Truthhauler Kalukithino](ThalaneTruthhaulerKalukithino.md) set foot in what would become his smithy, he hated it. Stone for safety, yes, room for storage, a place above to sleep, all these aspects were more than acceptable. But 7 foot ceilings meant he had to stoop.

"It's awfully small," he muttered to the little beaked lady who would be collecting the rent.

"You're awfully large," she replied in Truthhauler's own tongue. It was a phrase he'd never heard in the language he'd left behind so long ago.

He signed the papers.

***

Only a day passed before Truthhauler began coveting a new space. He tracked down his landlady flitting about near the docks.

"Why didn't you offer me the space down two doors from mine?" He used the king's speech, though he'd learned it as an adult. "It's the taller, and holds only boxes to my knee. I will move them for you."

She looked up at his face and used the king's speech back. "That space is rented. As is the one between you. As is yours. Only one of those is up to you to change."

He sighed. The high place would be perfect. But if he remained its neighbour he could surely take it over in the future.

"Thank you. Be sure to tell me when it becomes available."

She waggled her head in the way birdfolk do when they agree.

---

Many days passed. Truthhauler set up his smithy and began to work, always aware of the perfect spot two doors down where he wouldn't be stooping or bumping his head. 

His business took some time to get established, eventually settling on large farm implements rather than anything more delicate. Truthhauler told potential customers that he could make them weapons and armour, but his tools of war quickly developed a reputation for being ill-fitting and cumbersome. His shovels and plows were judged much more favourably.

And so as time went on, Truthhauler's farm implements sold, sometimes well, sometimes less well, and he became more and more established, but never really happy. Once in a while, more often in the cold winters, he would ask his little feathered landlady about moving into the other spot and she would always tell him she was satisfied with the rent it was currently making her, so he should just make sure he could keep on paying his. (He often wondered how the rent was paid, since he so rarely saw anyone enter or leave.)
